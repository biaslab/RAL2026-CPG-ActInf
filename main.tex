\begin{abstract}
Central pattern generators with attitude controllers produce signals that coordinate leg motion, allowing legged robots to walk robustly. Its parameters are tuned to the robot body and to the environment in which it operates. Whenever an event occurs that alters the robot's dynamics, the generator parameters may not be optimal again. Under suboptimal parameters, the robot may become unstable and eventually fall. To prevent this, we propose an agent that triggers a parameter updating phase when an uncertainty-weighted prediction error crosses a threshold. The threshold is not tuned, but derived from a false positive rate. In the triggered phase, the agent will propose candidate sets that it believes will restore predictions of level forward motion and restore optimality before it falls. Planning and action is performed by expected free energy minimization. We perform simulated experiments with sudden payload shifts, comparing the proposed active inference agent with state-of-the-art baselines in terms of travel distance. 
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Active inference, central pattern generators, legged robots, event-triggered control, online adaptation, expected free energy.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:introduction}

Legged machines are increasingly asked to work for long uninterrupted bouts in non-stationary conditions. Events such as payload shifts and actuator damage can lead to stumbling and falling.
Animals adapt to such events effortlessly and engineers have long pursued it by replicating their morphologies \cite{pettersen2017snake, dwivedula2025configbot}. Yet current leg controllers still fall far short. For example, Boston Dynamics' Spot relies on centralized, sensor-intensive control that is effective but complex and costly \cite{dwivedula2025configbot}.
Central pattern generators (CPGs) offer a simpler alternative.
In vertebrates, these spinal circuits generate the periodic signals of movements such as walking, freeing higher centers to modulate speed and gait through simple inputs \cite{goulding2009circuits, dickinson2000how}, and their decentralized architecture lowers command latency and bandwidth \cite{ijspeert2008special}.
The price is that the rhythm is shaped by only a handful of parameters, held fixed during walking, so a setting that is stable under one condition need not be stable under another.
When an event changes the condition mid-bout, staying upright requires re-tuning those parameters online.

Coupled nonlinear oscillators are biologically plausible and computationally efficient \cite{collins1994hard, ijspeert2008special}: mutual inhibition synchronizes the joints into coordinated gaits \cite{collins1994hard, sprowitz2008passive, righetti2008pattern}, and their limit cycles recover quickly from perturbations \cite{sprowitz2008passive}.
An open-loop CPG walks stably on flat ground, but a change in condition induces body rotations the rhythm alone cannot correct, so feedback must close the loop: contact feedback resynchronizes the oscillators to the actual footfalls \cite{righetti2008pattern}, and posture feedback corrects the trunk attitude.
Virtual model control (VMC) computes joint actions as if springs and dampers were attached to the body \cite{pratt2001virtual}; such springs pull the trunk level over terrain that defeats an open-loop CPG \cite{ajallooeian2013central}, and we adopt this attitude VMC as a reactive posture layer (Section~\ref{sec:vmc}).

Reactive feedback leaves the parameters untouched; a large or persistent change requires adapting them.
A damaged robot can search a precomputed behavioral repertoire by trial and error, recovering within a dozen physical trials \cite{cully2015robots}, while rapid motor adaptation infers a latent encoding of the condition from recent proprioception and adjusts a learned policy in a fraction of a second \cite{kumar2021rma}.
Each incurs a cost we avoid: trial and error executes every candidate on the compromised robot, and the learned policy needs extensive training.
A complementary line tunes gait parameters as online black-box optimization, fitting a Gaussian process surrogate to the parameter-score pairs seen so far and keeping proposals inside a trust region \cite{eriksson2019scalable}, but it too can only score a candidate by walking on it.

Deciding \emph{when} to act is a control problem in its own right.
Event-triggered control replaces a fixed update rate with a condition that fires once a monitored signal leaves a tolerance band, so effort is spent when the plant departs from expectation rather than on a schedule; paired with a Gaussian process, whose posterior variance reports where a model is still trustworthy, it has served remotely controlled vehicles for which every update is costly \cite{jang2025event}.
We inherit that structure but not its scarce resource: here physical trials are the constraint, not bandwidth, so the trigger gates a re-tuning whose cost would otherwise be paid in falls.

Both decisions follow from a single principle.
Active inference casts perception and control as inference under one generative model, with preferred outcomes as a prior over observations and actions chosen by minimizing expected free energy (EFE) \cite{friston2015active, dacosta2020active}.
That objective splits into a cross-entropy to the goal prior and an information gain \cite{koudahl2021on, kouw2023information}, so exploration follows from the agent's own uncertainty rather than a separately tuned acquisition rule, and the prior that scores a candidate gait also reports when the current one has drifted.

We contribute a prediction-error trigger that decides \emph{when} to re-tune the CPG, and a unified active inference agent that decides \emph{what} to re-tune to.
Screening candidates against a memory of failed gaits, it avoids the destabilizing on-robot trials that grid search, Bayesian optimization and extremum-seeking control require.
Under a persistent payload shift it accrues roughly a quarter of their falls and detects each shift sooner.

\section{PROBLEM STATEMENT}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $\xi = (\alpha, \dot{\alpha}, p, \dot{p})$ represent the orientation $\alpha \in SO(3)$, angular velocity $\dot{\alpha}$, position $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and linear velocity $\dot{p}$ of the trunk of a quadrupedal robot in a world frame.
The orientation $\alpha$ consists of roll $\alpha_r$, pitch $\alpha_p$ and yaw $\alpha_y$, and the position of a forward, a lateral and a vertical component $(p_x, p_y, p_z)$. 
Furthermore, let $\varphi$ be the robot's joint angles, $\dot{\varphi}$ the change in joint angles and $\tau$ the torques. Each foot $i$ additionally reports a binary contact state $c_i \in \{0,1\}$.
The map from ($\varphi, \dot{\varphi}, \tau$) to $\xi$ is unknown as it depends on the full rigid-body dynamics, the terrain and the switching foot-ground contact, none of which is available in closed form.
%
Locomotion is then the problem of finding torques that drive the base toward a desired state, such as a target forward velocity with roll and pitch near zero. 

\subsection{Central pattern generator}
\label{sec:cpg}

A CPG is a dynamical system that converges to a limit cycle, absorbing disturbances such as missteps due to uneven terrain.
%
We consider a CPG based on a network of four coupled Hopf oscillators \cite{rutishauser2008passive}.  For $i = 1, \dots, 4$, we define a Cartesian coordinate that evolves according to
\begin{align}
  \dot{z}_{1i} &= \beta_1 \big(\upsilon - (z_{1i}^2 + z_{2i}^2) \big) z_{1i} - \omega_i z_{2i} \, , \label{eq:xdot} \\
  \dot{z}_{2i} &= \beta_2 \big(\upsilon - (z_{1i}^2 + z_{2i}^2) \big) z_{2i} + \omega_i z_{1i}
               + \gamma \sum_{j=1}^4 k_{ij} z_{2j} + s_i \, , \label{eq:ydot}
\end{align}
where the $\beta$ are positive constants that control the convergence rate to the oscillator's limit cycle and $\upsilon$ is the squared limit-cycle amplitude.
Each Hopf oscillator has a stance and a swing phase that it balances through the parameter $\omega_i$;
\begin{equation}
	\omega_i = \omega_{\text{stance}} \varsigma(-b z_{2i})  + \omega_{\text{swing}} \varsigma(b z_{2i}) \, . \label{eq:omega_i}
\end{equation}
$\varsigma(\cdot)$ interpolates between the two frequencies based on the sign and magnitude of $z_{2i}$, with $b$ sharpening the balance.
%
The $k_{ij} \in \{-1,0,1\}$ indicate inhibitory or excitatory coupling between legs $i$ and $j$. The overall coupling gain $\gamma$ has been shown to reduce the dependence on initial conditions \cite{rutishauser2008passive}. 

The CPG incorporates a sensory feedback loop through $s_i$ in Eq. \ref{eq:ydot} \cite{righetti2008pattern}. Leg $i$ is classified as \emph{swinging} (i.e., no contact) when it exceeds a small positive threshold $\bar{z}$ and as \emph{standing} (i.e., with contact) when it is below a small negative one $\underline{z}$, with a hysteresis band in between;
\begin{align}
		\rho_i := \begin{cases}
		0 & |z_{2i}| > \bar{z} \\
		1 & |z_{2i}| < \underline{z} \\
		\rho_i^{-} & \text{otherwise}
	\end{cases}
  \label{eq:phase-classify}
\end{align}
where $\rho_i^{-}$ is the previous classification, so that the description latches the phase and prevents rapid switching. Resynchronization of the swing versus stance phase is triggered by a mismatch between the oscillator's phase $\rho_i$ and the measured foot contact $c_i$:
\begin{align}
s_i = \begin{cases} 
		\lambda \big( \omega_i z_{1i} - \gamma \textstyle\sum_{j} k_{ij} z_{2j} \big) & \text{if}\  c_i = \rho_i \\
	 	\eta \operatorname{sign}(z_{2i}) & \text{if}\ c_i \neq \rho_i \, . 
	\end{cases}
  \label{eq:feedback}
\end{align}
We refer to $\lambda$ as the phase match parameter and $\eta$ the phase mismatch. During phase match, $\lambda$ rescales the oscillator's own constants to $(1+\lambda)\,\omega_i$ and $(1-\lambda)\,\gamma$. During phase mismatch, $z_{2i}$ is driven away from zero, i.e., the leg holds its current swing or stance until it reaches a match again.

The oscillator states ($z_1,z_2$) map to the joint angles $\varphi$ through
\begin{align}
  \varphi^{\text{hip}}_i &=  \varphi^{\text{hip}}_0  +  A_{\text{hip}}\, z_{1i} \,, \label{eq:joint-map-hip} \\
  \varphi^{\text{knee}}_i &= \varphi^{\text{knee}}_0  -  A_{\text{knee}} \max( 0, z_{2i})  +  \Delta_i \,,
  \label{eq:joint-map}
\end{align}
with $\varphi^{\text{hip}}_0$, $\varphi^{\text{knee}}_0$ the angle origins and $A_{\text{hip}}$, $A_{\text{knee}}$ the swing amplitudes.
During swing ($z_{2i} > 0$) the knee flexes to lift the foot while the hip advances with $z_{1i}$, and during stance the knee holds its nominal extension.
The term $\Delta_i$ is the posture correction of Section~\ref{sec:vmc}.
%Joint-level position controllers track these references, producing the torques $\tau$ that realize the motion.
%
Overall, CPG parameters are collected into the vector
\begin{align}
    \theta = [\gamma, \omega_{\text{swing}}, \omega_{\text{stance}}, \eta, \lambda, A_{\text{hip}}, A_{\text{knee}}, b] \, .
    \label{eq:cpg-parameters}
\end{align}

\subsection{Attitude virtual model control}
\label{sec:vmc}

\begin{figure}[tb]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=.45\textwidth]{problem-payload3.png}
	\caption{Schematic of quadruped robot with base state $\xi$ being perturbed by leg motion. Events such as payload shifts alter which parameters are optimal. Arrows at feet indicate compensation by virtual attitude controller.}
	\label{fig:problem-payload}
\end{figure}

The CPG naturally absorbs disturbances and returns to its limit cycle, but there is no explicit mechanism to ensure the robot base maintains level.
We impose an attitude proportional-derivative (PD) controller to match the hip positions to a level reference plane, similar to a set of mass-spring-dampers on a car \cite{pratt2001virtual,ajallooeian2013central}.
%
For roll and pitch,we define:
\begin{align}
  c_r &= -\big( \kappa^{r}_{p}\, \alpha_r + \kappa^{r}_{d}\, \dot{\alpha}_r \big) \,, \label{eq:vmc-roll} \\
  c_p &= -\big( \kappa^{p}_{p}\, (\alpha_p - \bar{\alpha}_p) + \kappa^{p}_{d}\, \dot{\alpha}_p \big) \,, \label{eq:vmc-pitch}
\end{align}
with stiffness and damping gains $\kappa$.
Roll is regulated towards zero to combat transient rocking. Pitch is regulated towards a slowly adapting baseline $\bar{\alpha}_p$, an exponential moving average over recent pitch values, so the sustained pitch of an incline is tolerated rather than fought.
The commands distribute over the legs as:
\begin{align}
\Delta_1 &= \operatorname{clip}\big( \phantom{-}\ c_p + c_r ,\, \pm \, \delta_{\max} \big) \\
\Delta_2 &= \operatorname{clip}\big( \phantom{-}\ c_p - c_r ,\, \pm \, \delta_{\max} \big) \\
\Delta_3 &= \operatorname{clip}\big( -c_p + c_r ,\, \pm \, \delta_{\max} \big) \\
\Delta_4 &= \operatorname{clip}\big( -c_p - c_r ,\, \pm \, \delta_{\max} \big) \, ,
  \label{eq:vmc-distribution}
\end{align}
where the index runs over front left, front right, back left and back right, and $\delta_{\max}$ bounds the correction.
%A positive roll $\alpha_r$ banks the trunk to the right and a positive pitch $\alpha_p$ raises the front, so the sign on $c_p$ is positive for the front pair and the sign on $c_r$ is positive for the left pair.
The knee angle sets the effective leg length, so the legs on a sinking side of the trunk extend to push that corner up while those on the rising side shorten.

\subsection{Shifts in parameter optimality}
\label{sec:optimality-shift}

Let $e$ denote the \emph{operating condition} of the robot in the world.
It covers extrinsic factors such as terrain incline and friction, together with intrinsic ones such as the center of mass of the base.
Running the controller with parameters $\theta$ under condition $e$, with the attitude feedback of Section~\ref{sec:vmc} active, induces a distribution $p(\xi \given \theta, e)$ over the base state. Our desired state is expressed as a goal distribution over $\xi$, parameterized as a Gaussian distribution:
\begin{align} \label{eq:goal-prior}
	p(\xi \given \xi_{\star}, \Sigma_{\star}) = \mathcal{N}(\xi \given \xi_{\star}, \Sigma_{\star}) \, ,
\end{align}
where $\xi_{\star}$ represents the target and $\Sigma_{\star}$ indicates permissible deviation around that target. 
Our locomotion objective is then written as a cross-entropy between the induced distribution and the goal distribution:
\begin{align}
    J(\theta, e) =  \mathbb{E}_{p(\xi \given \theta, e)} \Big[ -\ln p(\xi \given \xi_{\star}, \Sigma_{\star}) \Big] \, ,
    \label{eq:criterion}
\end{align}
In practice the expectation is a time average over an evaluation window on low-pass-filtered angles, so that the rhythmic rocking of the gait is not penalized.
We can now define a set of CPG parameters that is optimal with respect to an operating condition $e$:
\begin{align}
    \theta_{\star}(e) = \underset{\theta}{\arg \min}\; J(\theta, e) \,.
    \label{eq:terrain-optimum}
\end{align}

Crucially, $\theta_{\star}(e)$ is a function of the environment and the robot's body.
Optimizing Eq.~\ref{eq:criterion} under differing terrain, load or actuator torque yields distinct parameter vectors, each most stable for its tuned condition. Thus, a change in condition relocates the optimum, $ \theta_{\star}(e_{\text{old}}) \neq \theta_{\star}(e_{\text{new}})$.
Examples of such changes include the robot stepping onto a new surface, a payload that slides off or an actuator that short-circuits (Figure \ref{fig:problem-payload}).

Because $\theta$ is held fixed, a shift leaves the controller suboptimal and the attitude deviations compound: as the trunk tilts, recovery soon demands more than the bounded feedback of Eq.~\ref{eq:vmc-distribution} can supply, and locomotion fails.
Resuming stable walking therefore requires two decisions, \emph{when} the optimum has moved and \emph{what} to re-tune $\theta$ toward. This has to be done quickly enough to prevent instabilities from increasing.

\section{Active inference agent} \label{sec:agent}

The active inference agent consists of a probabilistic generative model that predicts observations, updates a probability distribution over parameters after each observation, and plans actions to reach a target based on expected free energy minimization \cite{kouw2023information}. The agent makes a noisy observation $\mathbf{y}_k$ of the robot state $\xi_k$ at time $k$, and acts by modulating the CPG parameter vector $\theta$. While the agent is event-driven, the CPG is always running and generates joint angle setpoints which are turned to servo torques by a PD controller (see Figure \ref{fig:system-org}).

\begin{figure}[tb]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=.35\textwidth]{figures/system-org2.png}
	\caption{Overview of information flow: the agent observes base state and generates CPG parameters, which are turned into oscillator states and joint angle setpoints. These are picked up by a PD controller and turned into torques. Torques are based to the servomotors and the robot changes base state. The PD controller operates fastest, then the CPG and lastly the agent.}
	\label{fig:system-org}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Probabilistic model and belief updating}
We propose a probabilistic model based on multivariate autoregression with exogenous input (MARX). Let $\bar{\mathbf{y}}_k = [\mathbf{y}_{k-1}, \dots \mathbf{y}_{k-m_y}]$ be the recent history of base states, of dimension $D_y$ each, and let the joint angles $\varphi_k$ be incorporated as an exogenous signal.
A second exogenous channel carries what the agent has learned about the gait itself. A set of Gaussian process regression models maps the CPG parameters $\theta$ to each of the base state dimensions, fitted to a data set $\{(\theta_j, \mathbf{y}_j)\}_{j=1}^N$ of the gaits tried and the outputs they produced. As such, it constitutes a memory that will prevent a repeated use of $\theta$'s that lead to a fall. The models are fitted separately and then held fixed, so at any tick of the clock their stacked prediction $\mathbf{u}_{\theta}$ is a Gaussian distributed random variable. Collecting the three channels in a regressor $\mathbf{x}_k = [\bar{\mathbf{y}}_k^\top,\, \varphi_k^\top,\, \mathbf{u}_{\theta}^\top]^\top$, the evolution of $\mathbf{y}_k$ is described as a Gaussian distribution over autoregressive coefficients $A$ and process noise precision $W$:
\begin{align}
    p(\mathbf{u}_{\theta}) &= \cN\big(\mathbf{u}_{\theta} \given g(\theta), S(\theta)\big) \, , \label{eq:gp-input} \\
    p(\mathbf{y}_k \given \mathbf{x}_k, A, W) &= \cN\big(\mathbf{y}_k \given A^\top \mathbf{x}_k, W^{-1}\big) \, , \label{eq:marx}
\end{align}
where $g(\theta)$ collects the posterior means of the maps and the diagonal $S(\theta)$ their posterior variances.
The gait map thus enters as an input carrying its own uncertainty rather than as a known mean offset, and the block of $A$ reading it is estimated online.

We place a matrix-normal-Wishart prior on the pair \cite{nisslbeck2025factor},
\begin{align} \label{eq:mnw-prior}
    p(A \given W)  &=  \mathcal{MN}\big(A \given M_{k-1}, \Lambda_{k-1}^{-1}, W^{-1}\big) \\
   p(W) &= \mathcal{W}\big(W \given \Omega_{k-1}^{-1}, \nu_{k-1}\big) ,
\end{align}
writing $\Lambda$ for a precision over the rows of $A$, and $(\Omega, \nu)$ for a scatter matrix and degrees of freedom.

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:mnw}
Let $\mathbf{u}_{\theta}$ be distributed as in Eq.~\ref{eq:gp-input}, independently of $(A, W)$, and write $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k = [\bar{\mathbf{y}}_k^\top, \varphi_k^\top, g(\theta)^\top]^\top$ for the mean regressor and $\Sigma_{\theta}$ for $S(\theta)$ padded with zeros onto the remaining blocks. Then the matrix-normal-Wishart distribution minimizing variational free energy after observing $\mathbf{y}_k$ has parameters
\begin{align} \label{eq:mnw-update}
    \Lambda_k &= \Lambda_{k-1} + \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k^\top + \Sigma_{\theta} \, , \nonumber \\
    M_k &= \Lambda_k^{-1}\big(\Lambda_{k-1} M_{k-1} + \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k \mathbf{y}_k^\top \big) \, , \nonumber \\
    \Omega_k &= \Omega_{k-1} + \mathbf{y}_k \mathbf{y}_k^\top + M_{k-1}^\top \Lambda_{k-1} M_{k-1} - M_k^\top \Lambda_k M_k \, , \nonumber \\
    \nu_k &= \nu_{k-1} + 1 \, .
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
The proof is in the Appendix. The alteration is confined to the second moment of the regressor, where the Gaussian process covariance rides as a ridge on the block of $A$ that reads the gait map: a prediction the map is unsure of is attenuated rather than trusted, and the variance thereby left unexplained accumulates in $\Omega_k$ as process noise.

Marginalizing $A$ and $W$, and matching moments over $\mathbf{u}_{\theta}$, produces a multivariate location-scale T-distributed posterior predictive distribution:
\begin{align} \label{eq:predictive}
	p(\mathbf{y}_k \given \bar{\mathbf{y}}_k, \varphi_k, \theta) = \mathcal{T}_{\bar{\nu}_k} \big(\mathbf{y}_k \given \mu_k(\theta), \Sigma_k(\theta)\big) \, ,
\end{align}
whose degrees-of-freedom parameter is $\bar{\nu}_{k} = \nu_k  - D_y + 1$ and mean and a scale matrix is
\begin{align} \label{eq:predictive-moments}
    \mu_k(\theta) &= M_k^\top \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k \, ,  \\
    \Sigma_k(\theta) &= \frac{1 + \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k^\top \Lambda_k^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k + \tr\big(\Lambda_k^{-1}\Sigma_{\theta}\big)}{\nu_k - D_y - 1}\,\Omega_k + M_k^\top \Sigma_{\theta} M_k \, . \nonumber
\end{align}
The mean carries the memory of which $\theta$'s fell, while a $\theta$ the map has not seen inflates the scale through the two $\Sigma_{\theta}$ terms, so the epistemic value driving exploration below is inherited from the Gaussian process rather than supplied by a separate acquisition rule.

\subsection{Planning and inferring action}
When adaptation is triggered (Section~\ref{sec:trigger}), the agent calculates the Expected Free Energy of its predicted output under the goal prior \cite{kouw2023information},
\begin{align} \label{eq:EFE}
    &G(\theta) = - \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\!\big|I + \Sigma_k(\theta) \Sigma_{\star}^{-1}\big|}_{\text{information-seeking}}  \\
	&\quad \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\xi_{\star} \! - \! \mu_k(\theta)\big)^{\!\top} \Sigma_{\star}^{-1} \big(\xi_{\star} \! - \! \mu_k(\theta)\big) \! +\! \tfrac{1}{2}\tr\!\big(\Sigma_{\star}^{-1}\Sigma_k(\theta)\big)}_{\text{goal-seeking}} \,, \nonumber
\end{align}
The goal-seeking term seeks to match the mean and variance of the predictive distribution to the goal distribution. The information gain term favours elements of $\theta$ for which the predictive variance is larger than the goal variance as those represent epistemic uncertainty to be reduced. The agent will select the action that minimizes EFE:
\begin{align}
	\hat{\theta} =  \underset{\theta}{\arg \min}\  G(\theta)
\end{align}
Safety is not a separate constraint but a consequence of the goal prior. A gait that the memory associates with falling predicts outputs far from $\xi_{\star}$, which inflates the goal-seeking term and is avoided while the agent is still exploring.

\subsection{Triggering events based on prediction error}
\label{sec:trigger}

Re-tuning $\theta$ at every step is wasteful and potentially destabilizing, so the agent should adapt only when necessary. We characterize this need as an uncertainty-weighted prediction error on the orientation of the base. The prediction error is defined as the cross-entropy between the attitude marginal of the one-step prediction of Eq.~\ref{eq:predictive}, with location $\mu_k^{\alpha}(\theta)$ and scale $\Sigma_k^{\alpha}(\theta)$, and the attitude marginal of the goal prior. We desire a level base, so the orientation marginal of $\xi_{\star}$ is $0$, and the goal prior variance on orientation $\Sigma_{\star \alpha}$ encodes the permissible deviation around it. The prediction error is:
\begin{align} \label{eq:trigger-err}
    \epsilon_k(\theta) &= H\big[\mathcal{T}_{\bar{nu}_k}\big(\mathbf{y}_k^{\alpha} \given \mu_k^{\alpha}(\theta), \Sigma_k^{\alpha}(\theta)\big)\,\big\|\,p(\alpha \given 0, \Sigma_{\star\alpha})\big] \\
    &= \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_k^{\alpha}(\theta)^{\top} \Sigma_{\star\alpha}^{-1}\mu_k^{\alpha}(\theta) + \tfrac{1}{2}\tr \big(\Sigma_{\star\alpha}^{-1}\Sigma_k^{\alpha}(\theta)\big) + \text{const.} \,, \nonumber
\end{align}
It is small while the trunk is predicted level and grows when a perturbation drives it to tilt, requiring no model of the perturbation and no signal of when it occurs.
The monitor watches the gait that is currently running, so we drop the dependence on $\theta$ below.
Accumulating its excess over a reference level $\epsilon_0$ in a one-sided cumulative sum (CUSUM),
\begin{align} \label{eq:trigger-cusum}
    S_k = \max\big(0,\; S_{k-1} + \epsilon_k - \epsilon_0 \big) \,,
\end{align}
fires an event when $S_k$ exceeds a bound $h$, the reference level $\epsilon_0$ being the per-step surprise tolerated as noise.
The increment needs no scaling, since $\epsilon_k$ is already uncertainty-weighted: the scale $\Sigma_k^{\alpha}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:predictive} discounts a deviation by how much the autoregression expects the trunk to vary, so $\epsilon_k$ arrives smoothed and stays in nats.
Because the CUSUM integrates over time, it registers the sustained mismatch that a perturbation creates while rejecting the stride-frequency ripple that dominates a one-step residual at $100$\,Hz.

Event logic keeps a single perturbation from drawing more than a single correction.
The monitor is armed only after a sustained healthy interval past a grace period, so the transient following a recovery is never mistaken for a fresh perturbation.
Once a gait is applied, the accumulator is reset and held at zero for a settle window covering the ramp-in and one scoring hold, after which the monitor latches for the remainder of the event.
Reading uprightness alone is what lets the trigger \emph{quiet}: once the robot is upright again it falls silent, even under a recovered gait that walks slower or must crab sideways to absorb an asymmetric fault.
The agent thus stops adapting when it has restored balance, rather than chasing a nominal speed it may no longer reach.
The forward-velocity preference is not discarded, since it stays in the control objective of Eq.~\ref{eq:EFE}, which keeps seeking speed among the gaits that hold the trunk level.
Sensing and control therefore read one goal prior in two ways: its attitude marginal decides \emph{when} to act, and the full prior decides \emph{what} to do.

%\subsection{Setting the threshold from the cost of adapting}
%\label{sec:threshold}

The bound $h$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:trigger-cusum} need not be tuned, because the accumulator carries units.
Since $\epsilon_k$ is a cross-entropy in nats, $S_k$ is the free energy forgone, in nats, by holding the incumbent gait since the excursion began, so $h$ is a \emph{price} rather than a sensitivity.
Adapting carries a price of its own: writing $p(\theta) = \cN(\theta_k, \Upsilon^{-1})$ for a prior over gait parameters centered on the incumbent, a switch to $\theta_k + \Delta\theta$ costs
\begin{align} \label{eq:switch-cost}
    C(\Delta\theta) = -\ln p(\theta_k + \Delta\theta) + c = \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\theta^{\top}\Upsilon\,\Delta\theta \,,
\end{align}
paid once at the switch, whereas $\epsilon_k$ is a rate incurred afresh at every step the incumbent is held.
A threshold on $\epsilon_k$ itself thus compares a rate to a one-time cost, and is either tripped by transients or, set honestly, silent until the robot is already failing.
Accumulating first removes the inconsistency, and the bound becomes the price,
\begin{align} \label{eq:threshold}
    h = C(\Delta\theta) \,,
\end{align}
turning Eq.~\ref{eq:trigger-cusum} into a payback test: the agent switches once the value forgone by not switching repays what switching costs, after a delay that shortens as the mean excess of $\epsilon_k$ over $\epsilon_0$ grows.

%Equation~\ref{eq:threshold} considers only the subspace the gait parameters can steer, so it is a \emph{lower} bound on the principled threshold.
%At the operating point of Section~\ref{sec:exp-payload}, a switch is priced at $h = 0.125$\,nats, whereas the gaits the agent actually applies lie some twelve prior standard deviations out; pricing that realized move would delay the trigger by a median of $10.8$\,s and silence it in most bouts.
%The realized move is large because the search explores, not because correction demands it, and pricing instead the correction that the belief itself deems necessary, through its control gain, is left to future work.

Calibration in the experiments below rests on the reference level $\epsilon_0$, the only quantity still set by hand.
Over the $150$\,s of settled nominal walking $\epsilon_k$ stays below $\epsilon_0$, so every increment of Eq.~\ref{eq:trigger-cusum} is negative; the accumulator is pinned at zero by induction and no false alarm is possible on that sample, whatever $h$ is.
The bound $h$ then only imposes a short persistence requirement once a perturbation lifts $\epsilon_k$ past $\epsilon_0$.
%Beyond the observed support the guarantee weakens from certainty to an exponential rate, and $h$ is what sets it.

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:fpr}
Write $T = \inf\{k : S_k > h\}$ for the firing time.
Suppose that the excesses $\epsilon_k - \epsilon_0$ are independent and identically distributed with negative mean, and that some $\theta_{\star} > 0$ solves $\E\big[e^{\theta_{\star}(\epsilon_k - \epsilon_0)}\big] = 1$.
Then an unperturbed run of unbounded length fires with probability at most
\begin{align} \label{eq:lundberg}
    \Pr(T < \infty) \leq e^{-\theta_{\star} h} \,,
\end{align}
so a false-alarm budget $a$ is met by any $h \geq \ln(1/a)/\theta_{\star}$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is given in the Appendix.
The threshold is thus fixed twice over, and not by a sensitivity dial: Eq.~\ref{eq:threshold} prices it against the cost of switching, and Eq.~\ref{eq:lundberg} prices it against the false alarms one is willing to tolerate.
The two agree at the operating point of Section~\ref{sec:exp-payload}.
For Gaussian $\epsilon_k$ with healthy mean $\mu$ and spread $\sigma$ one has $\theta_{\star} = 2(\epsilon_0 - \mu)/\sigma^2$, which at the measured $\sigma = 0.025$\,nats is $240$\,nats$^{-1}$, giving $e^{-\theta_{\star} h} = 9\times10^{-14}$.
That is optimistic, since $\epsilon_k$ is strongly autocorrelated at $100$\,Hz, with a lag-one correlation of $0.92$ and a long-run variance $8.9$ times the marginal one; substituting the long-run variance for the one-step generating function gives $\theta_{\star} \approx 108$\,nats$^{-1}$ instead.
A budget of $a = 10^{-6}$ then demands $h \geq 0.128$\,nats, within a few percent of the $0.125$\,nats that the switching price sets independently.
The transient following a recovery reaches several hundred standard deviations and is kept from firing by the arming logic of Section~\ref{sec:trigger} rather than by $\epsilon_0$.

No matching guarantee is claimed against missed detections.
It would require the post-change increments to have positive mean, whereas the payload shift of Section~\ref{sec:exp-payload} raises the mean of $\epsilon_k$ only $0.023$\,nats above healthy against the $0.075$\,nats offset, so the accumulator still drifts downward on average and fires through excursions of a heavy-tailed statistic rather than through drift.
This suffices in practice, since every event in Section~\ref{sec:exp-payload} is detected, but we treat the false-negative rate as an empirical matter throughout.

\section{EXPERIMENTS}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the active inference agent on a Laikago quadruped in PyBullet, under a shifting trunk payload.
The agent runs with the attitude controller of Section~\ref{sec:vmc} active and adapts only the high-leverage subset of the parameter vector $\theta$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:cpg-parameters}, within fixed bounds throughout, while adaptation is gated by the prediction-error trigger of Section~\ref{sec:trigger}.
We compare it to holding the incumbent gait $\theta_{\star}(\text{flat})$ without adapting.
Control runs at $\Delta t = 0.01$\,s with a target forward velocity of $0.5$\,m/s.
Each perturbation event is scored by the criterion of Eq.~\ref{eq:criterion} over a $10$\,s hold window with the ramp-in excluded, and gait switches are ramped in over $0.3$\,s so that the gait never jumps.
We report that criterion on the attitude marginal of the goal prior and the forward-speed term separately, as distance covered.
The two are reported apart because no gait in the searched subset advances under this payload, the clairvoyant oracle included: with the speed term folded in, the criterion is nine parts speed deficit and ranks the oracle last, which measures the perturbation rather than the adaptation.

Adaptation runs asynchronously with the simulation, as it would on hardware.
The controller steps the physics at $100$\,Hz on one thread while each gait proposal, the Expected Free Energy minimization of Eq.~\ref{eq:EFE} with its Gaussian process fit included, is computed concurrently off the control loop.
A proposed gait takes effect only once that computation returns, so the wall-clock cost of the optimizer appears as a control latency over which the robot keeps walking on the un-adapted gait, rather than as a frozen clock.
Each bout being paced to real time, a slower optimizer is charged honestly, through the extra ground covered before its gait engages, and we report this compute latency alongside the detection latency below.

\subsection{Sudden payload shifts}
\label{sec:exp-payload}

The scenario perturbs the mass distribution of the robot rather than its actuation.
The Laikago carries an $8$\,kg payload rigidly attached above its trunk, whose own mass is $10$\,kg, and at random intervals the payload shifts off the sagittal plane, $0.215$\,m laterally and $0.20$\,m to the rear, ramped over $1$\,s so that the change is a sustained bias rather than an impulse.
The offset then persists: once engaged it is held indefinitely, so the trunk bias compounds until the robot either falls or settles into a gait that tolerates the load.
This offset sits at the edge of recoverability.
Holding the incumbent gait $\theta_{\star}(\text{flat})$, the robot tips over on $85\%$ of shifts after a median of $33$\,s under the load, yet a re-tuned gait can stay upright indefinitely, so the perturbation rewards adaptation without being hopeless.
The bout is non-episodic and runs for $300$\,s: whenever the robot falls it is stood upright and the payload recentered, and after $2$ to $8$\,s the shift recurs.
A controller that never adapts therefore meets the same perturbation over and over, whereas one that finds a tolerant gait simply holds it and walks on, so an agent that remembers earlier outcomes should stop repeating the parameter choices that fell.

At each detected shift the agent proposes a gait by minimizing the Expected Free Energy of Eq.~\ref{eq:EFE} over its control map, favoring parameters whose predicted outputs lie near the goal while avoiding the regions its memory associates with falling.
It searches only the high-leverage subset $\{\gamma, \omega_{\text{swing}}, \eta, \lambda, b\}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:cpg-parameters}, and the memory persists across recurrences within the trial, so the map fills in as the shift repeats.
Adaptation is triggered by the cross-entropy criterion of Section~\ref{sec:trigger}: the MARX dynamics belief, updated online from the trunk outputs and the measured joint angles, drives the CUSUM once its one-step prediction departs the goal, and re-arms once the robot has recovered after each recenter.

Three baselines are online optimizers that can judge a candidate gait only by running it: Latin-hypercube sampling (grid search), online Bayesian optimization, and extremum-seeking control, which dithers each parameter at a distinct frequency and demodulates the event score into a gradient estimate \cite{killingsworth2006extremum}.
Extremum seeking is the sharpest form of the difficulty, since even its exploratory dither must be executed on the already-loaded robot.
The fourth is a clairvoyant oracle that jumps straight to a pre-fit tolerant gait, an upper anchor on any parameter switch.

\subsection{Results}
We report the \emph{cumulative} falls that each method accrues over a bout rather than a per-event rate (Fig.~\ref{fig:payload-falls}).
Under no adaptation, the robot falls $5.18 \pm 0.18$ times per bout (mean $\pm$ s.e.m.), never adapting and tipping over on $85\%$ of recurrences.
Our agent instead falls only $0.41 \pm 0.08$ times, finding a tolerant gait after fewer than one fall on average and within the first $20$\,s, after which it holds that gait for the rest of the bout.
The three online optimizers fall in between, at $1.49 \pm 0.08$ (grid search), $1.59 \pm 0.09$ (Bayesian optimization) and $1.94 \pm 0.05$ (extremum seeking) falls per bout, each significantly worse than our agent (Mann-Whitney on per-seed fall counts, $p < 10^{-26}$).
They fall more because each can assess a candidate only by executing it on the already-shifted robot, where a poor candidate tips it over rather than merely scoring badly.
Extremum seeking is worst of the three, significantly worse than both grid search ($p \approx 6 \times 10^{-9}$) and Bayesian optimization ($p \approx 3 \times 10^{-6}$), even though its proposal is the cheapest to compute: its dither is by construction a perturbation applied to the loaded robot, so the exploration it needs in order to estimate a gradient is paid for in falls.
A clairvoyant oracle never falls, confirming that a recoverable gait exists within the searched subset.

%Triggering and proposing are both prompt enough to run online.
The goal-prior trigger detects the shift in $0.93$\,s on average, against $1.26$ to $1.38$\,s for the prediction-error monitors driving the other arms, with no false alarms in any arm.
Under the asynchronous protocol the gait proposal costs a median of one control step, since a tolerant gait is usually found before any Gaussian process fit is needed, rising to $1.74$\,s in the tail once the map is being fit.

The agent also stops re-tuning once a tolerant gait is in place, adapting exactly once per shift event across all one hundred bouts, so it does not chatter (Fig.~\ref{fig:payload-trigger}).
%Two measurements qualify what produces that restraint.
The attitude prediction-error does not return to its healthy baseline: it settles about $17\%$ above the pre-shift level, and comes within $10\%$ of baseline in only $5\%$ of bouts, since a load held off-center is never quite free.
The accumulator does re-cross its threshold later in most bouts, and no second adaptation follows because the monitor latches for the remainder of the event.
%The restraint is therefore a property of the event logic rather than evidence that the residual mismatch has vanished, and whether re-tuning on that residual would help is untested.

Scored by the criterion itself, much the same ordering appears without a fall ever being counted (Fig.~\ref{fig:payload-comparison}).
Our agent reaches $-1.11 \pm 0.03$\,nats against $-0.93 \pm 0.01$ for the fixed gait, the three optimizers falling between at $-0.98$ (grid search), $-0.99$ (Bayesian optimization) and $-1.02$ (extremum seeking), and the oracle at $-1.27$, against a floor of $-1.29$ for a trunk held exactly level.
The margin over each optimizer is significant (Mann-Whitney on per-seed means, $p < 10^{-11}$), whereas the three do not order among themselves as their fall counts do: extremum seeking scores best of them here while falling most, the criterion charging the tilt that precedes a fall rather than the fall itself.
%What the criterion adds is therefore corroboration from the prediction-error side, that the arms which fall less are also the ones held closer to the goal throughout.

At this offset the tolerant gaits are more compromised than the fall counts alone convey (Fig.~\ref{fig:payload-comparison}).
Every adapting arm settles into a gait that walks slowly \emph{backward} under the load, at $-0.12$\,m/s for our agent and $-0.14$\,m/s for the three optimizers, against a $0.5$\,m/s forward target.
The clairvoyant oracle is the most retrograde of all at $-0.22$\,m/s, which is the informative part: the pre-fit gait that never falls is itself a backward one, so within this parameter subset no gait both tolerates the offset and makes forward progress.
The benefit here is thus strictly falls avoided, and reaching a gait that walks forward under the load is capped by the gait parameterization, as we discuss next.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{DISCUSSION}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results separate two aspects of online adaptation that are easily conflated: deciding \emph{when} to change the gait and deciding \emph{what} to change it to.
The prediction-error trigger resolves the first, reading uprightness rather than speed and re-arming to catch a recurring sequence, without a model of the perturbation.
The second is where the danger lies, since grid search, Bayesian optimization and extremum seeking can judge a candidate gait only by walking on it.
Extremum seeking makes the point most sharply, its dither being a deliberate perturbation of the loaded robot that costs more falls than the two search methods it is cheaper than.
Our agent instead screens each candidate against a learned map with an explicit memory of past falls, and it does so even though the CPG is already stabilized by the attitude controller, so the benefit is complementary to the reactive posture loop rather than a substitute for it.

A strong limitation of this work is that the evaluation is in simulation, over a single intrinsic perturbation and a modest number of recurrences.
More fundamentally, how much adaptation can recover is capped by the gait parameterization itself: under this payload offset no gait in the searched subset both tolerates the load and advances, the clairvoyant oracle itself walking backward at $0.22$\,m/s, so every online method settles somewhere between it and a standstill.
Staying upright and making progress are thus in tension here, and our agent, like the others, resolves that tension toward staying upright.
All arms pay the same price, so the comparison stays clean, but adapting \emph{without} surrendering the locomotion objective is left open, and likely requires a richer control representation rather than a better search within this one.
Validating on hardware, and across a wider range of perturbations, are natural next steps.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{CONCLUSIONS}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We augmented a CPG of coupled Hopf oscillators with an attitude virtual model controller and studied when and how to re-tune its gait parameters online as the operating condition changes.
A prediction-error trigger decides when to adapt from a sustained loss of performance, and a unified active inference agent decides what to change by selecting parameters from a learned model of the body's response, with a persistent memory of past falls, rather than by physically trialing candidates.
On a shifting trunk payload, the agent learns within a single bout to stop selecting the gaits that fall, recovering without the destabilizing trials that grid search, Bayesian optimization and extremum seeking require.
A single goal prior serves both decisions, so no separately tuned detector is required.
How far this recovery generalizes, and how much the gait parameterization itself caps it, remain open.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mnw}}
\label{appx:belief-updating}
Marginalizing $\mathbf{u}_{\theta}$ out of Eq.~\ref{eq:marx} costs conjugacy: it leaves a covariance $W^{-1} + A^\top \Sigma_{\theta} A$ that filters the gait map's uncertainty through the very coefficients being estimated. We therefore keep $\mathbf{u}_{\theta}$ explicit and minimize variational free energy over the structured factorization $q(A,W)q(\mathbf{u}_{\theta})$, with $q(\mathbf{u}_{\theta})$ clamped to Eq.~\ref{eq:gp-input}; clamping is what fitting the maps offline and holding them fixed buys, in that the gait map is read by the observation rather than re-estimated from it. Free-form minimization over the remaining factor leaves
\begin{align*}
    \ln q^{\star}(A,W) = \E_{q(\mathbf{u}_{\theta})} \big[ \ln p(\mathbf{y}_k \given \mathbf{x}_k, A, W) \big] \\ + \ln p(A \given W) p(W) + c \, .
\end{align*}
The regressor enters the log of Eq.~\ref{eq:marx} only through $\mathbf{x}_k \mathbf{x}_k^\top$ and $\mathbf{x}_k \mathbf{y}_k^\top$, on which it depends linearly, and $\mathbf{x}_k$ is $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k$ with its third block replaced by $\mathbf{u}_{\theta}$. The expectation therefore passes onto those statistics alone,
\begin{align*}
    \E [\mathbf{x}_k \mathbf{x}_k^\top] = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k^\top + \Sigma_{\theta} \, , \qquad \E [ \mathbf{x}_k \mathbf{y}_k^\top ] = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k \mathbf{y}_k^\top \, ,
\end{align*}
and conjugacy is retained: the update is the known-input one, run at a second moment inflated by $\Sigma_{\theta}$. Adding the logs of the priors in Eq.~\ref{eq:mnw-prior} and collecting the terms in $A$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \tr \big[ W \big( A^\top \Lambda_k A - A^\top \Lambda_k M_k - M_k^\top \Lambda_k A \big) \big]
\end{align*}
with $\Lambda_k$ and $\Lambda_k M_k$ as claimed, so completing the square leaves the matrix-normal exponent $\tr [ W (A \! - \! M_k)^\top \Lambda_k (A \! - \! M_k) ]$ and a remainder $-\tr[W M_k^\top \Lambda_k M_k]$ that joins the terms free of $A$ to form $\Omega_k$. Matching the exponent of $|W|$ contributed by the likelihood, the matrix-normal normalizer and the Wishart prior against the Wishart form fixes $\nu_k = \nu_{k-1}+1$.~$\blacksquare$

Evaluating the completed square at $A = M_k$ resolves the scatter increment into three positive semi-definite contributions,
\begin{align*}
    \Omega_k - \Omega_{k-1} = (\mathbf{y}_k - M_k^\top \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k)(\mathbf{y}_k - M_k^\top \bar{\mathbf{x}}_k)^\top \qquad \\ + \, (M_k - M_{k-1})^\top \Lambda_{k-1} (M_k - M_{k-1}) + M_k^\top \Sigma_{\theta} M_k \, ,
\end{align*}
so $\Omega_k$ remains a valid scatter matrix. The first is the prediction residual, the second the distance the belief was forced to travel, and the third the gait map's own uncertainty read through the coefficients: what the map cannot pin down is booked as process noise rather than as evidence about $A$.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:fpr}}
\label{appx:fpr}

Let $X_k = \epsilon_k - \epsilon_0$, so that Eq.~\ref{eq:trigger-cusum} reads $S_k = \max(0, S_{k-1} + X_k)$ from $S_0 = 0$, and let $R_n = X_1 + \dots + X_n$ sum the same increments without the floor at zero.
Unrolling the recursion gives $S_n = \max_{0 \leq j \leq n}(R_n - R_j)$, the climb since the walk's lowest point: clipping at zero forgets everything before the last dip.
The increments being i.i.d., reversing their order changes nothing, so that climb has the law of the walk's highest point in $n$ steps.
Firing is therefore the event that the walk ever gets $h$ above its start.

The increments have negative mean, so a climb to $h$ is a run of luck.
Rescale the walk as $Z_n = e^{\theta_{\star} R_n}$: the condition $\E[e^{\theta_{\star} X_k}] = 1$ makes every step multiply $Z$ by a factor averaging one, so $Z$ is a fair game worth $1$ on average, and stays fair if stopped at the first time $\tau$ the walk exceeds $h$.
Stopped there it is worth at least $e^{\theta_{\star} h}$, and something averaging one reaches that value only that rarely,
\begin{align*}
    1 = \E[Z_{\tau \wedge n}] \geq e^{\theta_{\star} h}\,\Pr(\tau \leq n) \,.
\end{align*}
Letting $n$ grow gives Eq.~\ref{eq:lundberg}, and solving $e^{-\theta_{\star} h} \leq a$ gives the budget form. \hfill$\blacksquare$
